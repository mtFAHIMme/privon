Privacy Policy

Your files stay yours.

Effective: October 3, 2026 Applies to: Privon for Android

Privon is built on one rule: your files never leave your device. The app declares no permissions, collects nothing, and has no way to transmit anything even if it wanted to. This page spells out exactly what that means.

The short version

  • Privon declares 0 permissions in its manifest — no internet, no storage access.
  • Your files, filenames, and passwords are never collected, logged, or transmitted.
  • Encryption and decryption happen entirely on your device, in memory.
  • There are no ads, no analytics, and no third-party SDKs of any kind.

01 — Information we collect

Nothing

Privon has no accounts, no sign-in, and no server. There is no backend to collect data on, and no place for data to be stored even if collection were attempted. Every feature — selecting files, entering a password, encrypting, decrypting — happens locally.

02 — How your files are handled

Read, encrypted, written back — all on-device

When you pick files, Android's Storage Access Framework hands Privon temporary read access to exactly the files you chose — nothing else on your device becomes visible to the app. Each file is streamed through the cipher in your device's memory and written back out as a new encrypted file in the folder you select.

Nothing is copied to a cache, uploaded, or retained after the operation finishes. Once you leave the screen, Privon is holding nothing.

03 — How your passwords are handled

Used, stretched, discarded

Your password exists only in the app's memory long enough to be stretched into an encryption key through PBKDF2. It is never written to disk, never stored in preferences, and never cached in any form. When the encryption or decryption completes, the password and the derived key are cleared from memory.

04 — Device permissions

Zero, by design

Privon's manifest declares no permissions at all. Not INTERNET, not READ_EXTERNAL_STORAGE, not anything else. This means:

  • Uploading your files or passwords is technically impossible — the OS itself would block the attempt.
  • The app cannot see any file on your device except the ones you explicitly hand it through the system file picker.
  • There is no analytics SDK, no crash reporter, and no ad library silently requesting its own permissions.

05 — Third-party services

None

Privon does not integrate any third-party service, advertising network, or analytics SDK. There is nothing embedded in the app that contacts the outside world, because there is no permission for it to do so.

06 — Password loss

No recovery is possible — by design

Because Privon never sends, stores, or backs up your password, there is no recovery service, no reset link, and no way for anyone — including us — to unlock a file if the password is lost or forgotten. The files encrypted with that password cannot be recovered.

This is not a limitation we can fix without breaking the app's privacy promise. Store your passwords somewhere safe before you encrypt anything important.

07 — Cross-device notes

What happens between old and new phones

Android only supports SHA-256 for PBKDF2 from Android 8.0 (API 26) onward. Privon detects this and picks the appropriate hash for your device, recording the choice inside the encrypted file itself.

The practical effect: files encrypted on Android 7.1 or older decrypt fine on Android 8+, but files encrypted on Android 8+ cannot be decrypted on Android 7.1 or older — those devices simply cannot compute SHA-256 in PBKDF2. Privon reports a clear error rather than failing silently.

08 — Children's privacy

No data means no data from anyone

Since Privon collects nothing from any user, there is no personal information to collect from children either. The app is safe for users of any age from a data-privacy standpoint.

09 — Changes to this policy

Updated here first

If a future version of Privon ever needed to handle data differently — for example, if a new feature required a new permission — this page would be updated before that version shipped, with a new effective date at the top.
